New Interfaces for Musical Expression (NIMEs) are an important field of research for making musical interfaces more accessible, expressive, creative, and fun. In real-time musical performance, NIMEs provide new ways for musicians to interact with digital systems, expanding the possibilities for improvisation, collaboration, and musical exploration. Music is a fundamental aspect of human culture and self-expression, and improving the ways people can interact with it can increase inclusion and participation in this important aspect of human culture. Additionally, as AI-driven creative tools become more sophisticated, they have the potential to redefine artistic collaboration between humans and machines, opening new pathways for artistic expression. By improving the capabilities of NIMEs, we can not only enhance artistic possibilities, but also contribute to a deeper, more accessible, and more human-centered approach to digital music interaction. By augmenting or transforming traditional instruments, or even introducing entirely new modes of interaction, NIMEs allow performers to push the boundaries of human-computer interaction in music. Real-time interactive NIMEs are a subgroup which can interact with human performers in expressive ways, making them particularly valuable in live performance contexts. However, designing a system that can both understand and contribute meaningfully to a live musical performance remains a challenging task, requiring a balance between structured musical coherence and the flexibility of creative improvisation. This is a problem when developing interactive musical prediction systems which can improvise with a human in a call-and-response fashion, and is the focus of our research.

Recent developments in NIMEs which can be applied in real-time musical performance have broadly fallen into two categories. Heuristic based approaches break down musical harmony and structure symbolically into discrete components which can be logically reasoned with. By converting the expression of a human performer into this symbolic language, a NIME can then dynamically interact with the performer by performing calculations in this logical space~\citep{Fu2023MCTS}. This includes rule-based methods, where hardcoded musical constraints direct composition, and search based techniques, which explore the space of future musical directions, while being guided by heuristics. These methods are excellent for generating structured, coherent, and theoretically sensical musical outputs. However, they suffer from restrictions on the breadth and complexity of musical expression, which often leads to robotic and less creative sounding results~\citep{Anders2011ConstraintSurvey}.

In contrast, other methods have used recurrent neural networks (RNNs) trained on human performance to repeatedly predict the most likely next step in a performance. When used for NIMEs, these techniques solve many of the issues of heuristic-based approaches, as they are formulated to continuously predict the most human sounding continuation of a musical performance. However, these approaches aim for only the most likely next step, often leading to human-like, but very predictable and repetitive musical outputs. These is usually mitigated by adding randomness to the prediction process, by way of increased temperature, softmax sampling, or random sampling using Mixture Density Networks (MDNs). While adding randomness to the prediction process can make the performance more creative, it is a very arbitrary way to add this creativity and diversity, and has the tendancy to very quickly lead to incoherence if the randomness is too high~\citep{Martin2018RoboJam,Martin2019IMPSY}.

This motivates the question of whether a more guided approach than the addition of pure randomness to the prediction process could be used, and such a guided approach is what we present in this thesis. Our hybrid system, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:intro}, uses both heuristic and mixture density recurrent neural network (MDRNN) based approaches, with a search algorithm being used to guide the choice of outputs from the gaussian mixture model (GMM) of output likelihoods provided by an MDRNN prediction. From making repeated MDRNN predictions, and treating these predictions as a domain of actions within monte carlo tree search, a tree of possible simulated musical continuations is generated. Then, from the measurement of structural parameters of the music, such as harmonic consonance, rhythmic uniformity, and pitch variation (TODO: make these our actual parameters once the system design is complete), a heuristic measures the level of conformity in each simulated musical continuation to important musical qualities of the piece. Finally, these heuristic measurements are backpropogated through the search tree and used to guide the next musical output toward a choice which is consistent with the previous state of the performance. This allows for the system to maintain the benefit of MDRNNs, which is the ability to generate more creative and human-like outputs, without sacrificing musical coherence, and in this way better imitates the thought process of a human performer.

We apply our research method to the development of a new prediction algorithm for IMPSY~\citep{Martin2019IMPSY}, an interactive musical prediction system which uses an MDRNN to continue human improvisation in a call-and-response manner, aiming to generate improvisation which coherently carries on from the human performer. IMPSY is notably unique in that it predicts control gestures for an instrument, instead of the musical output itself. Currently, IMPSY randomly samples from a GMM output at each step to mitigate the predictability standard RNNs. Our research aims to guide this sampling instead through the method we have described, and which can be seen in Figure~\ref{fig:intro}.

\section{Methodology}
This section provides a brief summary of the aim of our research and the terminology we will use throughout the thesis. It then explains the methodology guiding the system design and evaluation processes.

We aim to extend the IMPSY system~\citep{Martin2019IMPSY}, also referred to as MDRNN-IMPSY to differentiate it from our new system, by modifying the prediction algorithm to use a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) based approach. This new system will be referred to as MCTS-IMPSY. The MDRNN prediction algorithm will be used to generate an action space of potential next notes from a given state, and by using this action space within the expansion phase of MCTS, a tree of potential musical continuations can be generated.

The methodology guiding the choice of approach to research is based on two notable findings from the literature. The first of these is the split between heuristic and machine learning based approaches to generative music, and particularly the notable difference in outcomes when each of these approaches is used in isolation. Heuristic and rule-based approaches are excellent at generating coherent music which adheres to established musical structure, but lack the expressiveness and creativity of modern machine learning systems. In contrast, machine learning based approaches are capable of generating more human-like and creative outputs, but often at the cost of coherence and structure. However, approaches combining both of these methods for music generation tasks are extremely limited in research, with no research being found combining both approaches for interactive musical systems. The second notable finding is that in other fields, such as robot motion planning, the combination of heuristic and machine learning based approaches has been shown to be extremely effective for improving the accuracy of a system by guiding probabilistic outputs of neural networks with search. In this field, a very similar combination of MCTS and neural networks to our system design have advanced the field of self-driving cars~\citep{Lei2021KBTree}, air combat simulations~\citep{Zhang2022AirCombat}, and more. This suggests that a similar methodology could be effective for interactive musical prediction systems. These two findings led to the development of a novel system design where rule-based approaches can be added as component heuristics to a larger combined heuristic which guides the search process of MCTS, itself defined on the action space generated by the predictions of an MDRNN.

For evaluating the system, our methodology is based on the aim of combining the strengths of both heuristic and machine learning based approaches, while limiting the weaknesses of each. Since our baseline is MDRNN-IMPSY, which is a machine learning based approach, the goal of our system is to improve musical coherence, structure, and predictive accuracy, while maintaining the expressiveness and creativity of the original system. Hence, the evaluation will be split into two parts. The first part will be a quantitative evaluation of the system, where we will compare the predictive accuracy of MCTS-IMPSY to that of MDRNN-IMPSY. The second part will be a qualitative evaluation of the system based on recorded improvisations with the system, where the playability, expressiveness, and creativity of MCTS-IMPSY will be compared to that of MDRNN-IMPSY. This evaluation will be conducted using both a hand crafted dataset of personal improvisations, to test the system for the practical use case IMPSY was originally designed for, and a larger dataset of over 1000 folk music melodies, to test the system for a more general use case.

\section{Research Questions}
This thesis aims to address the following questions:

\textbf{1. What are the strengths and weaknesses of heuristic and machine learning based approaches in NIMEs, and how can they be effectively combined?}

In Chapter 2, we will review the literature on NIMEs, including both heuristic and machine learning based approaches. We will discuss the strengths and weaknesses of each approach, and how they can be combined to create a more effective system. We will also look into other fields where combinations of heuristic and machine learning based approaches have been successfully applied. This includes discussion of some specific papers with very similar approaches to ours, using MDRNNs to generate GMM outputs, and then using those GMM outputs as continuous action domains for MCTS. Chapter 3 then describes our implementation of the combined MDRNN and MCTS algorithm, and its integration into IMPSY. We will discuss the details of the algorithm, including each of our design choices and why we believe they are the best choices for our system.

\textbf{2. How can an effective heuristic be designed for MCTS-IMPSY to intelligently guide the search process?}

Chapter 3 will also describe the heuristic we have developed for measuring the coherence of a musical performance. Each musical parameter which goes into this heuristic is described in detail, including how it is calculated and why it is important.

\section{Thesis Structure}
This thesis consists of 7 chapters. After this introduction in Chapter 1, Chapter 2 covers the necessary background information which is important for understanding our research in context. This includes a brief overview of NIMEs more generally, as well as the development of both machine learning and heuristic based approaches to NIMEs. It also covers combinations of these methods which are similar to our research, but applied in different fields. Finally, it discusses IMPSY, the system our research expands upon, and its limitations which we aim to address. Chapter 3 details our system design and implementation process, including the novel algorithm we have developed for predicting the next step in a musical performance, and the details of each component in the heuristic used in this algorithm.  Chapter 4 describes the experiments we have conducted to test our system and fine tune meta-parameters important to the algorithms performance. Chapter 5 then details both quantitative and qualitative measures of evaluation we have used to assess the performance of our system. Chapter 6 discusses the results of our experiments and evaluation, comparing our system to existing approaches and noting its strengths, limitations, and future work which could be conducted to expand upon our research. Finally, Chapter 7 concludes the thesis by summarising our findings and discussing the implications of our research.
