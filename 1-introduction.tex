New Interfaces for Musical Expression (NIMEs) are an important field of research for making musical interfaces more accessible, expressive, creative, and fun. In real-time musical performance, NIMEs provide new ways for musicians to interact with digital systems, expanding the possibilities for improvisation, collaboration, and musical exploration. Music is a fundamental aspect of human culture and self-expression, and improving the ways people can interface with it can increase inclusion and participation in this important aspect of human culture. Additionally, as AI-driven creative tools become more sophisticated, they have the potential to redefine artistic collaboration between humans and machines, opening new pathways for artistic expression. Improving the capabilities of NIMEs not only enhances artistic possibilities, but also contributes to a deeper, more accessible, and more human-centered approach to digital music interaction. By augmenting or transforming traditional instruments, or even introducing entirely new modes of interaction, NIMEs allow performers to push the boundaries of human-computer interaction in music. Real-time interactive NIMEs are a subgroup which can interact with human performers in expressive ways, making them particularly valuable in live performance contexts. However, designing a system that can both understand and contribute meaningfully to a live musical performance remains a challenging task, requiring a balance between structured musical coherence and the flexibility of creative improvisation. This is a problem when developing interactive musical prediction systems which can improvise with a human in a call-and-response fashion, and is the focus of this thesis.

Recent developments in NIMEs which can be applied in real-time musical performance have broadly fallen into two categories. Heuristic based approaches break down musical harmony and structure symbolically into discrete components which can be logically reasoned with. By converting the expression of a human performer into this symbolic language, a NIME can then dynamically interact with the performer by performing calculations in this logical space~\citep{Fu2023MCTS}. This includes rule-based methods, where hardcoded musical constraints direct composition, and search based techniques, which explore the space of future musical directions, while being guided by heuristics. These methods are excellent for generating structured, coherent, and theoretically sensical musical outputs. However, they suffer from restrictions on their breadth of musical expression, which often leads to robotic and less creative sounding results~\citep{Anders2011ConstraintSurvey}.

In contrast, other methods have used recurrent neural networks (RNNs) trained on human performance to repeatedly predict the most likely next step in a performance. When used for NIMEs, these techniques solve many of the issues of heuristic-based approaches, as they are formulated to continuously predict the most human sounding continuation of a musical performance. However, these approaches aim for only the most likely next step, often leading to human-like, but very predictable and repetitive musical outputs. These is usually mitigated by adding randomness to the prediction process, by way of increased temperature, softmax sampling, or random sampling using Mixture Density Networks (MDNs). While adding randomness to the prediction process can make the performance more creative, it is a very arbitrary way to add this creativity and diversity, and has the tendency to very quickly lead to incoherence if the randomness is too high~\citep{Martin2018RoboJam,Martin2019IMPSY}.

This motivates the question of whether a more guided approach than the addition of pure randomness to the prediction process could be used, and such a guided approach is what is presented in this thesis. This guided approach is in the form of a hybrid system, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:intro}, which uses both heuristic and mixture density recurrent neural network (MDRNN) based approaches, with a search algorithm being used to guide the choice of outputs from the gaussian mixture model (GMM) of output likelihoods provided by an MDRNN prediction. From making repeated MDRNN predictions, and treating these predictions as the action space for each node (musical note) within Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), a tree of possible simulated musical continuations is generated. Then, from the measurement of structural parameters of the music; such as key signature, tempo, and interval probabilities; a heuristic is created which measures the level of conformity of each simulated musical continuation to established musical parameters in the piece. Finally, these heuristic measurements are backpropogated through the search tree and used to guide the next musical output toward a choice which is consistent with the previous state of the performance. This allows for the system to maintain the benefit of MDRNNs, which is the ability to generate flexible outputs which allow for more creativity, without sacrificing musical coherence.

This new hybrid system is then applied as a new prediction algorithm for IMPSY~\citep{Martin2019IMPSY}, an interactive musical prediction system which uses an MDRNN to continue human improvisation in a call-and-response manner, generating collaborative improvisations with a human performer. For clarity, IMPSY with this MCTS-based prediction architecture is referred to as MCTS-IMPSY, and with the original single MDRNN prediction architecture is referred to as MDRNN-IMPSY. This new prediction system for IMPSY is the first application of a search-based system with a neural network generated action space in an interactive musical system. IMPSY is notably unique in that it predicts control gestures for an instrument, instead of the musical output itself. IMPSY is also designed for users to be able to train their own small prediction models on their own performances, so that the system can imitate their musical style. Currently, IMPSY randomly samples from a GMM output at each step to mitigate the predictability standard RNNs. This thesis aims to guide this sampling instead through the described method. \section{Methodology}
The methodology guiding the choice of approach to this research is based on two notable findings from the literature. The first of these is the split between heuristic and machine learning based approaches to generative music, and particularly the notable difference in outcomes when each of these approaches is used in isolation. Heuristic and rule-based approaches are excellent at generating coherent music which adheres to established musical structure, but lack the expressiveness and creativity of modern machine learning systems. In contrast, machine learning based approaches are capable of generating more human-like and creative outputs, but often at the cost of coherence and structure. However, research into approaches combining both of these methods for music generation tasks are extremely limited, with no research being found combining both approaches for any interactive musical system. The second notable finding is that in other fields, such as robot motion planning, the combination of heuristic and machine learning based approaches has been shown to be extremely effective for improving the accuracy of a system by guiding the probabilistic outputs of neural networks with search. In this field, very similar combinations of MCTS and neural networks to this system design have advanced the field of self-driving cars~\citep{Lei2021KBTree}, air combat simulations~\citep{Zhang2022AirCombat}, and more. This suggests that a similar methodology could be effective for interactive musical prediction systems. These two findings led to the development of a novel system design where rule-based approaches can be added as component heuristics to a larger combined heuristic which guides the search process of MCTS, itself defined on the action space generated by the predictions of an MDRNN.

The methodology for evaluating the system is based on the aim of combining the strengths of both heuristic and machine learning based approaches, while limiting the weaknesses of each. Since the baseline is MDRNN-IMPSY, which is a machine learning based approach, the goal of MCTS-IMPSY is to improve musical coherence, structure, and predictive accuracy, while maintaining the expressiveness and creativity of the original system. Hence, the evaluation will be split into two parts. The first part will be a quantitative evaluation of the system, where the predictive accuracy of MCTS-IMPSY will be compared to MDRNN-IMPSY. The second part will be a qualitative evaluation of the system based on recorded improvisations with the system, where the playability, expressiveness, and creativity of MCTS-IMPSY will be compared to that of MDRNN-IMPSY. This evaluation will be conducted using both a hand crafted dataset of personal improvisations, to test the system for the practical use case IMPSY was originally designed for, and a larger dataset of over 1000 folk music melodies, to test the system for a more general and structured use case.

\section{Research Questions}
This thesis aims to address the following questions:

\textbf{1. What are the strengths and weaknesses of heuristic and machine learning based approaches in NIMEs, and how can they be effectively combined?}

In Chapter 2, the literature on NIMEs will be reviewed, including both heuristic and machine learning based approaches. The strengths and weaknesses of each approach will be discussed, as well as how they can be combined to create a more effective system. This section will also look into other fields where combinations of heuristic and machine learning based approaches have been successfully applied. This includes discussion of some specific papers with very similar approaches to ours: using MDRNNs to generate GMM outputs, and then using those GMM outputs as continuous action domains for MCTS. Chapter 3 then describes the implementation of the combined MDRNN and MCTS algorithm, and its integration into IMPSY. The details of the algorithm, including each design choice, and an exmplanation of how an effective combination of approaches is developed, are in Section 3.

\textbf{2. How can an effective heuristic be designed for MCTS-IMPSY to intelligently guide the search process?}

Chapter 3 will also describe the heuristic developed for measuring the coherence of a musical performance. Each musical parameter which influences a component of the heuristic is described in detail, including musical and/or psychological research on its relevance to musical prediction and understanding in humans, and how it is calculated.


\textbf{3. Can MCTS-IMPSY improve on the prediction accuracy and playability of MDRNN-IMPSY, while maintaining creativity?}

In Chapter 4, the system is evaluated using both quantitative and qualitative measures. It will be compared to MDRNN-IMPSY for measures of both musical quality and improvisational prediction ability. Chapter 5 and 6 then also discuss the implications of this thesis for the future of NIMEs and human-computer interaction more broadly.

\section{Thesis Structure}
This thesis consists of 6 chapters. After this introduction in Chapter 1, Chapter 2 covers the necessary background information which is important for understanding this thesis. This includes a brief overview of NIMEs more generally, as well as the development of both machine learning and heuristic based approaches to NIMEs. It also covers combinations of these methods which are similar to MCTS-IMPSY, but applied in different fields. Finally, it discusses IMPSY, the system this thesis expands upon, and its limitations which MCTS-IMPSY addresses. Chapter 3 details the system design and implementation process, including the novel algorithm for sequential musical prediction, and the details of each component in the heuristic used in this algorithm. Chapter 5 then details both quantitative and qualitative measures of evaluation used to assess the performance of the system, and the results of this evaluation. Chapter 6 discusses the implications of these results, noting their broader impact and avenues of future work created, but also the limitations of this thesis. Finally, Chapter 7 concludes the thesis by summarising the findings of this thesis and directly answering the research questions posed in the previous section.
