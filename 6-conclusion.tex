\chapter{Conclusion}\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis presents a novel system design architecture for interactive musical prediction, which combines the strengths of both machine learning and rule-based approaches to generate coherent and expressive musical responses in real-time. By integrating Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with a Mixture Density Recurrent Neural Network (MDRNN), we have developed a system which combines the strengths of heuristic based music generation in structural and musical cohensive with the strengths of neural netowrk based systems in creative and expressive range, while mitigating the inverse weaknesses of each approach when used individually.

\subsection{Answers to the Research Questions}

In the introduction of this thesis, three research questions were posed, which we will now summarise the answers to as established by the findings within this thesis.

\textbf{1. What are the strengths and weaknesses of heuristic and machine learning based approaches in NIMEs, and how can they be effectively combined?}

Machine learning-based approaches, particularly neural networks like MDRNNs, excel in capturing complex patterns and generating a wide range of creative and expressive musical ideas. However, they often lack inherent mechanisms for ensuring structural or musical coherence, sometimes leading to incoherent or unpredictable outputs when solely relying on random sampling. Heuristic-based approaches, conversely, are strong in providing structural coherence and adhering to specific musical rules, but they may lack the flexibility and expressive range of machine learning models.

These two approaches can be effectively combined by leveraging the strengths of each. This thesis demonstrates that integrating a search algorithm like MCTS, guided by a heuristic, with a machine learning model like MDRNN, can mitigate the weaknesses of individual approaches. The MDRNN provides a rich space of possible musical outputs, while the MCTS, informed by musical heuristics, intelligently navigates this space to select musically coherent and expressive predictions. This combined approach allows for the creative exploration offered by neural networks to be tempered with the structural guidance of rule-based systems, resulting in a more powerful and nuanced musical prediction system. Prediction systems based on rule-based approaches or Markov models can have their prediction ability converted into component heuristics and integrated into neural network-based systems through MCTS.

\textbf{2. How can an effective heuristic be designed for MCTS-IMPSY to intelligently guide the search process?}

TODO edit gen answer:

An effective heuristic for MCTS-IMPSY is designed to mimic a human performer's real-time checks and balances during improvisation, ensuring that musically intuitive options are also coherent. The heuristic is primarily based on musical parameters that psychological research and musical studies have shown to be relevant to human perception and prediction of music. The algorithm is designed to be flexible, allowing for customization of the exact heuristics based on the instrument, performance goals, or specific musical styles/cultures.

The heuristic guides the Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) process, which incrementally and asymmetrically builds a search tree of possible future control inputs. Each potential musical output (node in the search tree) is assessed by the heuristic values of its explored future expansions. The MCTS algorithm iterates through selection, expansion, simulation, and backpropagation steps, using a modified Upper Confidence Bound for Trees (UCT) algorithm to balance exploration and exploitation. This ensures that the system focuses computational resources on the most promising musical paths that align with the defined heuristic, leading to intelligent guidance of the prediction process. All but one of the parameters used in the heuristic are generalizable to any instrument with continuous pitch and time dimensions, with the exclusion having the only additional limitation of a discrete tuning system, acting as a baseline for more specific configurations.
