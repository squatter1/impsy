\chapter{Conclusion}\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis presents MCTS-IMPSY, a novel prediction architecture for interactive musical prediction, which combines the strengths of both machine learning and rule-based approaches to generate coherent and expressive musical responses in real-time. By integrating Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with a Mixture Density Recurrent Neural Network (MDRNN) to define its action space, MCTS-IMPSY combines the strengths of heuristic based music generation in structural and musical cohesive with the strengths of neural network based systems in creative and expressive range, while mitigating the inverse weaknesses of each approach when used individually. MCTS replaced the stochastic sampling of probabilistic MDRNN outputs with an intelligent and guided selection process based on a set of musical parameter measurements which combine to form a heuristic. This is the first time that this approach to prediction has been applied to an interactive musical system. To conclude this thesis, a short summary of the key findings is provided, and then answers to the three research questions posed in the introduction will be given.

\section{Summary of Key Findings}

The evaluation, conducted using both quantitative and qualitative metrics across different datasets, demonstrates the effectiveness of an MCTS-enhanced approach. Evaluations of MCTS-IMPSY against the previous MDRNN-IMPSY system show a 67.5\% and 145.2\% improvement in prediction accuracy for each of the two datasets used. Qualitative analysis of improvisations performed with the system show that this increase did not come at the cost of expressiveness, with the playability and practical usefulness of the system being significantly improved with the new prediction architecture, particularly for more structured improvisations. This work represents a significant proof of concept for human-computer interaction as a whole, showcasing the potential of combining search algorithms with machine learning in interactive artistic systems such as NIMEs.

This research serves as a proof of concept for similar prediction architectures to be used in various fields. The customizability and generalisability of heuristic components in MCTS-IMPSY mean that the system can be adapted to various musical styles, instruments, and performance goals, offering a flexible foundation for future developments in interactive artistic systems. The implications extend beyond music, with potential use cases in generative music systems, gestural control, networked instruments, and even other creative domains. This thesis provides a solution to the common problem of balancing structure and coherence with creativity in AI-based prediction systems, with is a problem present in all of these fields.

Because of the function of this research in creating a generalisable and easily customisable prediction framework, their are a multitude of avenues for future work. User interfaces could be developed which allow users to adjust the parameters controlling music generation in real-time, user-generated parameters could be used as heuristics to guide search, the system presented in this thesis could be applied to other fields, and much more.  

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}

In the introduction of this thesis, three research questions were posed, which will now be answered based on findings established earlier in this thesis.

\textbf{1. What are the strengths and weaknesses of heuristic and machine learning based approaches in NIMEs, and how can they be effectively combined?}

Machine learning-based approaches, particularly neural networks like MDRNNs, excel in capturing the small patterns and details in music which humans build their musical intuition around, allowing them to generate a wide range of creative and expressive musical ideas. However, they often lack inherent mechanisms for ensuring structural or musical coherence, sometimes leading to incoherent or unpredictable outputs. Heuristic-based approaches, conversely, are strong in providing structural coherence and adhering to specific musical rules, but they may lack the flexibility and expressive range of machine learning models.

These two approaches can be effectively combined by leveraging the strengths of each. This thesis demonstrates that by searching a tree of potential musical continuations, with a neural network to generate the action space and a rule-based heuristic to guide the search process, a system that explores the rich and expressive space of musical possibilities generated by machine learning methods is created, while still focusing on musical paths which are musically coherent and follow the established parameters of the piece.

\textbf{2. How can an effective heuristic be designed for MCTS-IMPSY to intelligently guide the search process?}

An effective heuristic for MCTS-IMPSY is designed to mimic a human performer's real-time checks and balances during improvisation, ensuring that musically intuitive options are also coherent. The heuristic is primarily based on musical parameters that psychological research and musical studies have shown to be relevant to human perception and prediction of music. The algorithm is designed to be flexible, allowing for customization of the exact heuristics based on the instrument, performance goals, or specific musical styles.

In this thesis, musical parameters that have been stablished as useful include key signature, tempo, and the Markov models describing the probability of certain changes in pitch and duration over successive notes. These parameters are first measured while the human performer plays, and when they stop, a set of important musical features are established based on the current state of the music. For example, this could be that the current key signature is C major and the tempo is 120 BPM. Each clearly established musical feature then forms a component of the heuristic, which for each explored musical continuation, assigns a score based on how well the continuation fits with the established musical features. For example if a continuation contains multiple notes which are not in C major, it would be assigned a lower score. The heuristic is designed to be flexible and customizable, allowing for the addition of new parameters or the adjustment of existing ones based on the specific needs of the performance or the instrument being used. This flexibility allows for a wide range of musical styles and performance goals to be accommodated within the MCTS-IMPSY framework.

\textbf{3. Can MCTS-IMPSY improve on the prediction accuracy and playability of MDRNN-IMPSY, while maintaining creativity?}

Yes, MCTS-IMPSY significantly improves upon the prediction accuracy and playability of MDRNN-IMPSY, while successfully maintaining creativity. The evaluation demonstrates that the MCTS-enhanced system leads to 67.5\% increase in correct predictions for a dataset trained on my own improvisations, and a 145.2\% increase for a more structured and larger dataset trained on over 1000 folk melodies. Qualitatively, a structured improvisation test showed that MCTS-IMPSY increased playability due to more coherent outputs improving the ability to have meaningful call-and-response interactions. In freeform improvisations, the improvement was less pronounced due to the reliance of some heuristic components on specific structures like tempo and key signature. However, MCTS-IMPSY was preferred to MDRNN-IMPSY in this case too. Crucially, these improvements in prediction accuracy and coherence did not come at the expense of expressiveness and creativity. Instead, by refining musically incoherent predictions, the system in a sense fostered creativity by blocking non-sensical musical ideas in favour of creative expression which remained reasonable and coherent.
